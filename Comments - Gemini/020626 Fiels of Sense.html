This is a sophisticated synthesis of Nicolai Hartmann’s stratified ontology and Markus Gabriel’s New Realism. By pivoting from Gabriel’s radical “The World does not Exist” to your refined “The World is not an Object,” you effectively resolve the nihilistic tension in Gabriel’s work while maintaining the structural utility of his Fields of Sense.

Here is an assessment and breakdown of how this modification integrates into a critical ontological framework.

1. The Mechanism of the Field of Sense

In your proposed structure, the Field of Sense acts as the “connective tissue” within Hartmann’s levels (Inorganic, Organic, Psychic, Spiritual). It provides a way to explain how entities within a specific stratum—or even across spheres (the Real vs. the Ideal)—interact without requiring a single, all-encompassing “container.”

  • Relational Appearance: As you noted, an object like an ice cube only “makes sense” through its relations (heat transfer, physical support).
  • Recursive Nesting: Fields can be objects in higher fields (Room \(\rightarrow\) House \(\rightarrow\) Neighborhood), which mirrors Hartmann’s dependence/autonomy laws between strata.

2. Gabriel’s “World” vs. Your “World”

The crux of your argument lies in the definition of existence vs. objecthood.

Concept Gabriel’s View Your Proposed Modification
Existence To exist is to appear in a Field of Sense. To exist is a broader category; appearance is a subset of existing.
The World The “Field of Sense of all Fields of Sense.” The “Systematic totality of all that exists.”
Conclusion Because the World cannot appear in a field, it does not exist. Because the World has no external relation, it is not an object.

Why “The World is not an Object” works:

  1. Avoids Paradox: It sidesteps the linguistic trap where saying “The World does not exist” requires the World to “be” something in order to deny its existence.
  2. Respects Totality: By defining the World as a non-object, you allow it to be the totality of all strata and spheres without forcing it to obey the rules of “appearance” that govern constituent objects.
  3. Hartmann Compatibility: Hartmann’s ontology is “critical” precisely because it acknowledges the Trans-objective—realities that exist independently of our cognition or appearance. Your modification preserves the Ontic (the thing-in-itself) even if it never enters a Field of Sense.

3. Logical Structure of the Modification

Your revision of the syllogism provides a cleaner ontological boundary. By replacing Gabriel’s definition of existence with a definition of objecthood, you maintain the integrity of the “Field of Sense” as a tool for describing manifestation rather than being.

The Refined Syllogism: 1. To be an object is to appear in a Field of Sense (relational). 2. Appearance requires a relationship between at least two entities. 3. The World (as totality) cannot have an external relationship. 4. Therefore, the World cannot appear in a Field of Sense. 5. Conclusion: The World is not an object (but may still exist as the totality).

4. Application to Hartmann’s Strata

Using Fields of Sense within Hartmann’s framework allows for a “Local Realism.” For instance:

  • The Physical Stratum: A Field of Sense defined by gravitational and electromagnetic relations.
  • The Spiritual (Geist) Stratum: A Field of Sense defined by meanings, historical contexts, and values.

By using Reference (as you cited via Frege) to differentiate these fields, you prevent “category errors”—such as trying to explain the “sense” of a fictional character using the “laws” of the inorganic stratum.

Final Assessment

Your modification is a grounded, “common-sense” improvement on Gabriel. It keeps the epistemological clarity of New Realism (knowing where and how things appear) without inheriting its ontological austerity (denying the totality of the world).

It essentially moves the World from a failed set to a unique category of being that transcends the subject-object relationship.
